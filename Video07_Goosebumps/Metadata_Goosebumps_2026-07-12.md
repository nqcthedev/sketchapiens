Why do you get goosebumps? That prickle on your skin, the hair standing up on your arms, is one of the clearest ghosts in the human body. It's a reflex left over from human evolution, built for an animal covered in fur, still firing on a body that lost that fur about a million years ago. This is the ancient, evolutionary reason your body raises a coat it no longer has.

You're a mostly hairless human. So why does your body flex a hundred tiny muscles to fluff up... basically nothing? Because for your furry ancestors, this exact reflex did two life-or-death jobs: it trapped warm air to survive the cold, and it puffed them up to look bigger and scare off a predator. You kept the reflex. You just lost the fur that made it work.
