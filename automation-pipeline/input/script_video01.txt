# Source: Video #1 "Why Did Humans Lose Their Body Hair?"
Right now, you have roughly the same number of hair follicles as a chimpanzee.
Same follicles. Wildly different result.
They got a fur coat. You got goosebumps and a hoodie.
Almost every mammal on Earth is covered in fur.
We are one of the very few that walked away from it.
So this is the story of how humans became the naked ape.
And no, it wasn't because we got cold and invented clothes.
We gave up our fur on purpose, more than a million years ago.
And the reason is hiding in your skin right now.
Here's the part that messes with people.
We didn't actually lose our hair.
We have about five million follicles, the same as a great ape.
What changed is the hair itself.
Most of ours turned into tiny, thin, see-through wisps called vellus hair.
The factory is still running. It just makes a much smaller product now.
So technically, you are not hairless.
You are covered in hair you can barely see.
Which raises the real question.
Why would evolution take a perfectly good fur coat and shrink it to nothing?
To answer that, we go back about two million years, to a very hot problem.
Picture early humans on the African savanna, under a brutal midday sun.
We were not the fastest hunters out there. Not even close.
What we had was a weird superpower. We could keep going.
It is called persistence hunting.
We chased animals for hours until they overheated and collapsed.
But to do that without cooking ourselves, we had to dump heat fast.
And here is where bare skin wins.
Humans have millions of sweat glands across the body.
We cool down by sweating over the entire skin.
A thick fur coat would trap that sweat like a hot, wet blanket.
Anthropologist Nina Jablonski argues this is the core reason we went bare.
Less fur, more sweat, better cooling.
A body built to outlast its own dinner.
But this raises a tricky question. When did it actually happen?
Skin and hair do not fossilize, so how could we ever know?
Geneticist Alan Rogers found a clever workaround using a gene called MC1R.
Under fur, skin is pale, because the fur blocks the sun.
The moment you lose the fur, pale skin gets hammered by UV.
So dark skin should evolve right after the fur is gone.
Rogers dated the rise of dark skin to about 1.2 million years ago.
Which means the fur was already gone before that.
Evolution leaves receipts. You just have to know where to look.
