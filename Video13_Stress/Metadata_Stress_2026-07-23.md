## 2. DESCRIPTION (paste thẳng — 50 từ đầu nhồi keyword ngách)

Why does stress make you sick when an ancient human's didn't? A hunter fifty thousand years ago felt the same racing heart and the same flood of adrenaline and cortisol you do, but his stress switched on with the lion and switched off when it left. Yours never switches off. This is the real story of the human stress response, evolution, and why your Stone Age body treats a deadline exactly like a predator.

It isn't weakness and it isn't all in your head. The fight-or-flight alarm in your chest is the same wiring that kept your ancestors alive, built to spike for a few minutes and then vanish. In this video we break down how the amygdala pulls the alarm before you can think, why adrenaline and cortisol flood the body, why your inbox and your phone act like a lion that never leaves, how chronic cortisol quietly wears down your immune system and your sleep, and what Stanford neuroscientist Robert Sapolsky learned watching wild baboons on the African plains.

There's an honest section too. Acute stress still saves your life, not every illness traces back to cortisol, and the fix was never an app. It's the thing your ancestors had without thinking: a real threat that actually ends.
