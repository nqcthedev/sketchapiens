Winter nights in Ice Age Europe ran sixteen hours long, and the temperature fell past freezing
in the first hour. Every account of ancient humans and the cold asks the same question: how did
they stay warm. This one asks the question underneath it. How did anybody fall asleep at all.

Falling asleep is not something a body does by lying still. It starts when the small blood
vessels in the hands and feet open and the body pushes heat out through the skin. Cold does the
exact opposite, and it does it first. This video follows what that conflict means for a person
lying on frozen ground, and what it says about the real job of a fire.
