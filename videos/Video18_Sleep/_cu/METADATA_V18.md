Two things worth being straight about.

First: the sentinel study is one Hadza camp, twenty nights, in 2017. It shows the
mechanism works in living hunter-gatherers. It does not prove your ancestors had
it, and nothing can — nobody has measured a night from fifty thousand years ago.

Second: I cut a whole chapter out of this video. It was about "first sleep and
second sleep" — the idea that people used to sleep in two blocks with an hour
awake in the middle. The historical evidence for that is genuinely strong, but
Siegel's team looked for it in the Hadza, San and Tsimané and did not find it,
and they concluded in the paper that segmented sleep was probably not there
before humans moved north into Western Europe. It's a real finding, it just
belongs in a different video.

Sources are all in the description. If I got something wrong, tell me and I'll
pin the correction.
