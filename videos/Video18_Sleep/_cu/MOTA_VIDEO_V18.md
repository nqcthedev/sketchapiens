## PHẦN DÁN VÀO Ô MÔ TẢ

```
How Did Ancient Humans Sleep With No Door?

No door, no lock, no walls, and a leopard somewhere in the dark. This is what
actually happened when humans slept outside — when they went to sleep, how long
they slept, and who was keeping watch. The answer to that last one is not what
anyone expects.

HOW THIS WAS MADE

I picked this topic, read the papers, and wrote the script myself. Where the
evidence only goes so far, I say so in the video instead of rounding it up into
a better story. Every source is listed below so you can check any of it.

If I got something wrong, tell me in the comments and I will pin the correction.


📚 RESEARCH & SOURCES

▸ THE SENTINEL HYPOTHESIS — THE CORE OF THIS VIDEO
  Activity trackers were fitted to an entire Hadza community in Tanzania and
  recorded for 20 nights. Across all twenty nights there were only eighteen
  minutes when everybody was asleep at the same time. For 99.8% of sampled
  epochs at least one person was awake, with a median of eight people awake.
  Nobody was assigned to keep watch. The coverage came from age-related
  differences in chronotype.
  Samson, Crittenden, Mabulla, Mabulla & Nunn, "Chronotype variation drives
  night-time sentinel-like behaviour in hunter-gatherers", Proceedings of the
  Royal Society B, 284(1858): 20170967 (2017).

▸ HOW LONG THEY ACTUALLY SLEPT
  Three genetically, geographically and historically separate pre-industrial
  societies — the Hadza (Tanzania), San (Namibia) and Tsimané (Bolivia) — slept
  between 5.7 and 7.1 hours a night, averaging 6.4. All three went to sleep
  roughly three hours after sunset, and woke about an hour before sunrise.
  Sleep onset tracked falling temperature rather than falling light.
  Yetish, Kaplan, Gurven, Wood, Pontzer, Manger, Wilson, McGregor & Siegel,
  "Natural Sleep and Its Seasonal Variations in Three Pre-industrial Societies",
  Current Biology (2015).

▸ INSOMNIA
  Between 1.5% and 2.5% of people in those three societies reported trouble
  initiating or maintaining sleep, against 10-30% in industrialised countries.
  None of the three languages has a word for insomnia.
  Same source, Yetish et al. 2015.

▸ WHAT THEY TALKED ABOUT AFTER DARK
  Years of recorded conversations among the Ju/'hoansi of the Kalahari. Daytime
  talk was largely economic and social regulation — who is hunting where, which
  plants are ready, who owes what. After dark around the fire, more than 80% of
  talk was stories.
  Wiessner, "Embers of society: Firelight talk among the Ju/'hoansi Bushmen",
  Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, 111(39) (2014).
  DOI: 10.1073/pnas.1404212111

▸ SK 54 — SWARTKRANS, SOUTH AFRICA
  A partial hominin cranium recovered in 1949 from the Swartkrans Member 1
  deposits, roughly 1.9-1.8 million years old. It carries two carnivore
  puncture marks on the parietal bones whose location, size and spacing indicate
  a leopard. Recent work argues the specimen sits closer to Homo erectus than to
  Paranthropus robustus, which is why the video calls it "a relative of ours"
  rather than naming a species.
  Original description: C.K. Brain. Reassessment: "We the hunted", South African
  Journal of Science (2024).

▸ FIRST SLEEP AND SECOND SLEEP — WHY IT IS NOT IN THIS VIDEO
  Roger Ekirch spent sixteen years finding hundreds of references, across
  centuries, to a "first sleep" and a "second sleep" separated by an hour or
  more of wakefulness. In 1992 Thomas Wehr at the NIMH gave volunteers fourteen
  hours of darkness a night for a month and every one of them split their sleep
  the same way, with elevated prolactin during the waking hours.
  But Siegel's team looked for exactly this in the Hadza, San and Tsimané and
  did not find it, and concluded segmented sleep was probably not present before
  humans migrated into Western Europe. Wehr's fourteen hours of darkness is a
  European winter; the equator gets closer to twelve.
  Ekirch, segmented sleep research (Virginia Tech). Wehr, "In short
  photoperiods, human sleep is biphasic", Journal of Sleep Research (1992).


⚠️ WHAT THIS VIDEO DOES NOT CLAIM

The sentinel study is one community, twenty nights, in 2017. It shows the
mechanism works in living hunter-gatherers. It does not show that your ancestors
had it, and nothing can — nobody has measured a night from fifty thousand years
ago.

Even if they did have it, it was never the whole answer. Fire mattered. So did
numbers, and a spear within reach. How much each of those was worth, nobody can
put a figure on.

Somebody was awake almost all the time. Almost is not always. SK 54 is what
almost looks like.
```

## KHỐI "FIRST SLEEP" — VÌ SAO ĐỂ TRONG MÔ TẢ DÙ ĐÃ CẮT KHỎI VIDEO

Chương đó bị cắt sau review GPT vì **lỗi logic**: ngủ phân đoạn là kiểu ngủ *bên trong một người*, còn hiệu ứng lính gác là sự lệch nhịp *giữa nhiều người* — hai cơ chế khác nhau, không bắc cầu sang nhau được.

Nhưng để nó trong mô tả làm ba việc:
1. Đón đầu bình luận *"sao không nhắc tới two sleeps?"* — câu chắc chắn sẽ có
2. Cho thấy đã **đọc và cân nhắc** rồi mới bỏ, không phải không biết
3. Là **góc nhìn gốc** đúng nghĩa: nói ra một kết luận có nguồn mà gần như không clone nào biết — *giấc ngủ phân đoạn là hiện tượng phương Bắc, không phải của loài người*
