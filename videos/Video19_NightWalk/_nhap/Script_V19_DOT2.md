# V19 — ĐỢT 2: CHƯƠNG 2 (cái lạnh phá chính ca đêm ở chương 1)

```
The cold.
Get cold, and your body does something clever and, in this one situation, catastrophic.
To protect the heart and the brain, the blood vessels near your skin clamp shut.
Your hands go pale. Your feet go numb. That is the clamp.
All that blood has to go somewhere, so it is driven inward, into the core.
Suddenly the middle of you is holding more fluid than it was built to hold.
And the kidneys read that as a surplus, and start dumping it.
The name for this is cold induced diuresis, and you have felt it.
It is why standing outside in winter sends you looking for a toilet.
Here is the cruel part.
It used to be thought the body simply stopped making vasopressin when it got cold.
More recent work points somewhere worse.
The body keeps sending the instruction. The kidneys stop reading it.
The message is marked urgent. It sits there unopened.
Researchers still argue about the exact mechanism.
But the effect is not in doubt, and neither is the timing.
Cold means night.
Cold means no clothes worth the name.
Cold means the fire is the only warm thing for a hundred miles.
So the nights when walking away from that fire was most likely to kill you
are exactly the nights your body was most determined to send you out there.
That is not bad luck. That is a system working perfectly, in the wrong century.
Which finally makes the real question worth asking.
Not why they went out. We know why now.
What was waiting when they did.
```
