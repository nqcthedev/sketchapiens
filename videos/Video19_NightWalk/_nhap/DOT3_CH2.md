# V19 — ĐỢT 3: CHƯƠNG 2 — CÁI LẠNH PHÁ CA TRỰC

```
That system was working fifty thousand years ago too.
So what overrode it?
The cold did.
When you get cold, your body does something sensible that backfires here.
To protect your heart and your brain it clamps down the blood vessels near the skin.
That is why your hands go pale and your feet go numb.
All that blood has to go somewhere, so it gets pushed inward.
Suddenly your core is carrying more fluid than it usually does.
Your kidneys read that as a surplus and start getting rid of it.
The name for this is cold induced diuresis.
You have felt it every time standing outside in winter sent you looking for a toilet.
Now, the part researchers took a while to work out was not that it happens. It was why.
For a long time the assumption was that cold simply switched the vasopressin off.
The more recent work says something different, and worse.
The vasopressin is still being sent. The kidneys stop responding to it.
The night shift is still on duty. Nobody is taking its calls.
Researchers are still arguing about the exact mechanism, and it would be wrong to say it is settled.
But the effect is not in doubt, and the effect arrives at the worst possible moment.
Cold means night. Cold means the fire is the only warm thing within reach.
So on the coldest nights, the system holding a person beside that fire is the least reliable it ever gets.
Now put those two facts next to each other.
The cold decides whether you have to go outside.
It has no say in what is waiting when you do.
That was true for everyone who ever slept beside a fire.
But some people had far less say in it than others.
```
