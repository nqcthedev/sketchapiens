There is one more turn in this, and it runs the other way.
The moon does not only take the light away from the people.
It takes it away from the lion first.
Lions hunt by ambush.
Getting close without being seen is the whole method.
And a week of bright nights is very bad for that.
The researchers put it plainly.
Lions are hungriest just after the full moon, because the abundance of light just before and during the full moon limits their ability to hunt successfully.
So the bright half of the month is not a gift.
It is a delay.
People are safer while the moon is up, and the animal waiting past the treeline is getting steadily emptier the whole time.
Then the moon starts rising late.
The early evening goes dark.
And a hungry ambush hunter gets its hours back.
The safest nights and the deadliest nights are the same two weeks, in order.
One pays for the other.
None of this required anyone back then to understand it.
A pattern can kill people for a hundred thousand years without anybody working out that it is a pattern.
But there is something in the modern numbers that resists that.
