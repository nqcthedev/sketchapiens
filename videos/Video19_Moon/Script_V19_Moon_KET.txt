The moon is doing all of it right now.
It comes up as the sun goes down, once a month, and then loses the better part of an hour every night after that.
The dark stretch arrives on time.
None of that has ever needed a person to notice it.
Which brings it back to the walk down to the water, forty steps from camp, in the grey half hour.
Whoever made that walk did not know they were standing inside a cycle.
They may have known that some evenings felt worse than others, and stayed in.
They may have gone anyway, because water does not fetch itself.
The marks on the bone do not say.
What is certain is narrower, and much older than any of the counting.
For a very long stretch of human history, whether a person came back from that walk was decided by something nobody could reach, nobody could argue with, and everybody could see.
And it is up there tonight, on exactly the same schedule, over a world where nobody times the walk to the water by it any more.
