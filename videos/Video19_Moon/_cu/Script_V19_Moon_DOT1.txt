There is a night, somewhere in the last hundred thousand years, when nothing happens.
A small group is lying on bare ground under an overhang.
The fire has burned down to a red smear.
Nobody is keeping watch.
In the morning everyone gets up.
Ten nights later, same overhang, same people, same fire.
One of them does not.
Nothing on the ground had changed.
Something in the sky had.
What those people were thinking is gone.
That part does not survive.
What survives is that the two nights were not the same kind of night, and the gap between them was not small.
And it had been visible for weeks, to anyone who looked up.
This is about the only clock they had, and what it was counting.

Night is not one thing.
Under a clear full moon there is enough light to walk without putting a hand out.
Enough to make out the shape of a person at thirty paces.
Enough to notice that something low is moving through the grass, and to notice it early.
On a clear night with no moon, there is roughly a hundred times less of it.
Not a little darker.
Not twice.
About a hundred.
Same clearing.
Same trees.
Same distance down to the water.
Two completely different places, ten nights apart, and no one had to be told.
For a person, that gap is the difference between walking and feeling for the ground.
For everything that eats a person, it is the difference between a bad night at work and a very good one.
