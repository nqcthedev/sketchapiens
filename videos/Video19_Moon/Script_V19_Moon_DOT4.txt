In 2021 researchers put sleep monitors on three communities in Argentina.
All three were Toba and Qom people.
One had electricity around the clock.
One had power inside the houses and nothing past the doorway.
One had none at all.
The monitors tracked when people fell asleep, night after night, across the whole lunar month.
And the sleep moved with the moon.
On the nights before a full moon, when there is moonlight in the hours right after dark, people went to sleep later and slept less.
The effect peaked three to five days before the full moon itself.
The community with no electricity showed it most strongly, which is roughly what anyone would guess.
