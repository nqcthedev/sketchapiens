## 2. DESCRIPTION (paste thẳng — 50 từ đầu nhồi keyword ngách)

Why does your back hurt when an ancient human's didn't? A hunter fifty thousand years ago walked fifteen kilometres a day, carried a deer across his shoulders and slept on hard ground, and his back was fine. You stand up from a chair and your spine files a complaint. This is the real story of the human back, evolution, and the one modern object that broke it.

It isn't bad posture. It isn't just age. Your spine is the same four-million-year-old S-curve that hunter had, and it was never designed to be switched off. In this video we break down why lower back pain is the single biggest cause of disability on Earth, why the Hadza and the Tsimane barely deal with it, what disc-pressure research says about slouching, why your spinal discs have no blood supply and are literally fed by movement, and how the chair, an object barely two hundred years old, quietly retired a spine built to work all day.

There's an honest section too: discs really do wear, upright posture really does have trade-offs, and not every back pain is lifestyle. But for the ordinary ache most of us carry, the fix was never a thousand-dollar chair. It's the free thing your ancestors did without a single thought.
