Why are your feet so weak when an ancient human's weren't? A caveman ran across sharp rock, hot sand and cracked ground barefoot, every day for years, and his feet were fine. You step on one small pebble in the driveway and fold in half. This is the real story of ancient human feet, evolution, and how modern shoes quietly switched yours off.

It isn't flat feet. It isn't bad genes. Your foot is a two-million-year-old spring, and it has spent your whole life in a soft cast. In this video we break down the 3.66-million-year-old Laetoli footprints that prove the arch is ancient, the 17% of running energy your arch gives back for free, why the barefoot Hadza barely deal with bunions or plantar fasciitis, and how every "comfort" feature in a modern shoe is doing a job your foot used to do itself.

There's an honest section too: not every foot problem is the shoe's fault, and you can't undo thirty years of support in a weekend. But the fix was never a two-hundred-dollar shoe. It's the free thing your ancestors did without a single thought.
