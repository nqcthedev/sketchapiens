# CHƯƠNG 4 — HARAMAYA
```
There is one thing in this story nobody can measure, and it is worth saying out loud.
Nobody knows how well a fire actually worked.
There is no study of Paleolithic campfires and how many predators they turned back.
There never will be. You cannot rerun the Stone Age.
But you do not have to, because in some places that walk out of the light never stopped.
In the Haramaya district of eastern Ethiopia, three researchers spent two years counting what spotted hyenas were doing to people.
They finished in 2013.
They recorded twenty four attacks.
Half of those attacks happened while the person was going to the toilet in the evening.
The rest happened to people sleeping outdoors, or walking between villages after dark.
Between 2010 and 2012, twelve people in that district were killed.
Seven of them were children under the age of twelve.
Read that number again, because it is the whole argument.
The people least able to wait until morning were the people least able to survive not waiting.
That district has roads, a hospital, and electricity in some houses and not others.
What it shares with fifty thousand years ago is a much shorter list.
An animal that hunts at dusk, a shelter you have to leave, and no light on the path.
```
