## 2. DESCRIPTION

```
Ancient humans couldn't drink milk. Almost no adult mammal can. Human evolution
gave most of the world a body that shuts lactase off after weaning — and then
about 10,000 years ago, a prehistoric mutation broke that switch for some of us.
This is why you can drink milk made for babies, and why being lactose intolerant
was never the disorder.

Every mammal on earth quits milk. The enzyme that digests it, lactase, is
supposed to switch off once you stop nursing, because your body doesn't pay to
keep a tool it will never use again. That's the factory setting. It is still the
setting most adults on the planet are running.

Then the herds arrived. A copying error near the lactase gene jammed the switch
on for life — lactase persistence — and in a world of cattle it suddenly paid
for itself: clean calories, water you could trust, calcium all year round, from
a source your neighbours physically couldn't touch.

So the people who wrote the dictionaries were milk drinkers. They named their
own setting normal, and named the original one a disorder.

If you pour a glass and nothing happens, you're not the normal one. You're
carrying a young mutation stuck onto a Stone Age body.
```
